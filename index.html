Quantum optics · Rydberg atoms · Ultracold quantum matter

I am a theoretical physicist working at the interface of atomic physics, quantum optics, and quantum many-body theory. My research explores how light interacts with atoms, how Rydberg states can be controlled and trapped, and how correlations shape the dynamics of ultracold quantum systems.

About

My research experience spans postdoctoral work at the University of Oklahoma, USA, and the University of Haifa, Israel, following my PhD at the Indian Institute of Technology Kharagpur, India. I combine analytical insight with computational methods to investigate atomic properties, atom–light interactions, and interacting quantum gases.

I am interested in fundamental questions in quantum physics as well as their potential connections to precision spectroscopy, quantum simulation, quantum metrology, and sensing.

Research interests

Rydberg atoms and light

Optical forces, dynamic polarizability, magic wavelengths, polarization effects, and the interaction of Rydberg states with structured light.

Quantum optics and trapping

Light-induced atomic control, optical trapping, state-dependent energy shifts, and theoretical routes toward precision spectroscopy.

Ultracold many-body systems

Quantum dynamics, tunnelling, fragmentation, correlations, and transport in interacting Bose gases and bosonic Josephson junctions.

Coupled-cluster methods

Many-body approaches for multicomponent bosonic systems, including interspecies correlations and extensions to more complex mixtures.

Atomic structure and spectroscopy

Computational atomic properties, transition matrix elements, polarizabilities, and spectroscopy of atoms and ions.

Quantum technologies

Theoretical foundations relevant to quantum sensing, quantum simulation, and precision measurement with atomic systems.
